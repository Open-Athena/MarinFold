Where the contact maps go wrong

Many predictions find the right neighborhoods but choose the wrong detailed contacts. In the worst cases, the error is larger: local contact clusters replace the long-range pattern.

97 natural proteins · exp277, epoch 2 · step 479417 · 100 rollouts per protein · saved evaluation from 19 September 2026

55.8%Mean exact precision at R true contacts
81.2%Mean precision among the top L/5 contacts
93.9%True contacts seen in at least one rollout
64.0%Oracle ±2 correction, one-to-one

1. A bias toward local contacts

Long-range pairs are 67.6% of true contacts but 59.0% of top-R predictions. In the lowest-accuracy quartile this widens to 69.3% versus 41.2%. Missing off-diagonal traces are especially visible in 8arl_A and 7wz5_A.

2. “Nearby” is not “correct”

60.8% of wrong predictions lie within ±2 residues at both endpoints of some true contact. But fixing exact matches and pairing errors one-to-one with missed contacts raises the matched fraction only from 55.8% to 64.0%. Local shifts explain part of the gap.

3. Coverage does not ensure good ranking

About 94% of true contacts are sampled at least once, yet 29.3% get fewer than 10 votes. The highest-ranked L/5 contacts are much more accurate. This points toward weak support and ranking, alongside complete misses in the hardest proteins.

Interpretation, limits and the next experiment

These are descriptive observations, not a causal identification of a training defect. Marginal vote matrices cannot show whether contacts co-occur in the same rollout or form a physically consistent structure. Whole-map sample analysis is needed to distinguish mixing incompatible folds from errors inside individual maps.

Priorities suggested by these maps: investigate why long-range contact support collapses on the weakest proteins; test whether whole-map or geometry-aware selection can recover under-ranked blocks; measure local contact refinement separately from fold recovery. The oracle correction uses truth, so it is an upper bound and cannot be deployed as a predictor.

The published decontaminated-AFDB sequence-KNN reference scores 40.7% on eval-val (exp245/exp277); it was not rebuilt over the full native-plus-redesigned training mixture. Viral proteins score 48.0% (6 proteins), versus 56.3% for the 91 non-viral proteins; the small viral sample limits inference. The frozen exp260 low-MSA-depth natural subset has no eval-val members, so this report cannot speak to that subset.

What is being compared

  1. Checkpoint: contacts-v1-exp277-m2-p06-full-epoch2-from213072-1.5B, step 479417. Saved run v2-02; temperature 1.0, top-p 0.95, top-k disabled, 100 independently resampled prompts. No eval-val rollouts were capped.
  2. Truth: exp245's frozen pyconfind side-chain contacts, degree ≥0.001, with sequence separation ≥6. This is the benchmark's contact definition; it is not a Cα/Cβ 8 Å map. Only pairs of experimentally resolved residues are scored. Gray rows and columns are unknown, not negatives.
  3. Ranking: number of terminating rollouts containing the pair, with the original stable tie order. Top-R takes exactly R pairs, where R is that protein's true contact count. At this threshold precision and recall are equal. The true contact count is an evaluation device, not known at inference time. Top L/5 is more selective and usually covers far fewer true contacts.
  4. Validation: all 1,940 per-protein metric rows (97 × four ranges × five metrics) reproduce the exp277 source table to 1e−12. The long-range R-precision is 53.9%; that metric re-ranks and sets R within long-range pairs. The viewer's range filter does not recompute that metric.
  5. Near misses: ±2 means each endpoint may move at most two residues in sequence coordinates. Many-to-one proximity gives 80.7%, versus a uniform-random candidate expectation of 14.6%. The stricter 64.0% oracle keeps exact matches, then maximally pairs false positives with still-missed true contacts one-to-one. Neither tolerance score is a new model accuracy or evidence that such corrections can be inferred.
  6. Aggregation: headline percentages are unweighted means over proteins. This is an exploratory eval-val analysis. No eval-test proteins were analyzed, and no fresh model inference was run.
